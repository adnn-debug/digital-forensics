### 1. Purpose and Evidentiary Notice

This document establishes the scientific boundaries, algorithmic constraints, and legal limitations of the automated analytical tools provided by the **PS-05 Platform**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The automated analytical outputs generated by this platform (such as ELA heatmaps, spectrogram visual representations, and keyframe metrics) constitute **investigative heuristics and decision-support indicators**. Under the Federal Rules of Evidence and international forensic standards, **automated software output alone does not constitute dispositive, self-proving proof of tampering, authenticity, or forgery**. All outputs require interpretation and corroboration by a qualified digital forensic examiner.

### 2. Image Forensic Analysis: Error Level Analysis (ELA)

#### 2.1 Technical Mechanism
Error Level Analysis operates by intentionally re-saving an image as a lossy JPEG at a predetermined quality level (typically 90%–95%) and calculating the absolute difference between the original pixel matrix and the recompressed pixel matrix:

$$\Delta(x, y) = |I_{\text{original}}(x, y) - I_{\text{recompressed}}(x, y)|$$

The resulting difference matrix is scaled to enhance human visibility. In theory, regions that have been edited, pasted, or spliced from a source with different compression history will exhibit distinct error rates relative to the surrounding image.

#### 2.2 Inherent Limitations & False Positives
1. **High-Contrast Edge Sensitivity (False Positive):** High-frequency spatial transitions (such as sharp boundaries between dark and light objects, text overlays, or high-contrast textures) naturally produce higher mathematical error rates during Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) quantization. An examiner might misinterpret these natural high-error edges as spliced content.
2. **Smooth, Flat Color Surfaces (False Negative):** Smooth, low-entropy regions (such as clear blue skies, plain walls, or dark shadows) exhibit near-zero error regardless of whether they have been digitally altered. Splicing or digital airbrushing in flat areas cannot be detected reliably via ELA.
3. **Multiple Re-Saving Homogenization:** Every time a JPEG is opened and resaved, the quantization errors across the entire 8x8 block grid progressively homogenize. An image that has been saved 5–10 times will show uniformly low error levels, obscuring earlier local edits.
4. **Matching Compression Parameters:** If an attacker splices content from a source image that was originally compressed with the exact same quantization tables as the destination image, ELA will reveal no discernible discrepancy.
5. **Lossless Formats:** ELA is completely invalid when applied directly to native lossless formats (PNG, BMP, TIFF) unless those images were previously converted from lossy JPEGs.

#### 2.3 Courtroom Admissibility Caveats
- ELA **cannot** establish *who* performed an edit or *when* it occurred.
- ELA **cannot** distinguish between benign processing (such as camera firmware post-processing, lens distortion correction, or HDR tonemapping) and malicious semantic tampering (such as removing a person from a crime scene).
- In courtroom testimony under the *Daubert* standard, ELA must be presented alongside camera quantization matrix verification and raw metadata analysis.

### 3. Video Forensic Analysis: Codec Artifacts & Frame Extraction

#### 3.1 Technical Mechanism
The video analysis engine extracts container stream profiles via FFprobe and decomposes video streams into constituent Intra-coded frames (I-frames/keyframes) and temporal frame sequences using FFmpeg and OpenCV.

#### 3.2 Inherent Limitations & Codec Realities
1. **Inter-Frame Temporal Compression (H.264 / HEVC / AV1):** Modern video codecs do not store individual complete frames. They utilize Group of Pictures (GOP) architectures comprising:
   - **I-frames:** Complete, independent images.
   - **P-frames & B-frames:** Predictive frames storing only motion vectors and residual error deltas.
   Artifacts appearing in P- and B-frames (e.g., macroblock pixelation, motion ghosting, color smearing) are natural consequences of temporal compression and cannot be cited as proof of spliced video frames without analyzing the underlying macroblock prediction modes.
2. **Social Media Transcoding (The "WhatsApp/YouTube Effect"):** When videos are transmitted through consumer messaging platforms or social media networks, the platforms:
   - Strip all original EXIF and camera container metadata.
   - Force re-encoding with aggressive Variable Bitrate (VBR) profiles.
   - Convert frame rates (e.g., 60 fps to 30 fps or Variable Frame Rate - VFR), dropping frames in the process.
   These platform-induced alterations generate temporal discontinuities and compression artifacts that mimic malicious tampering.
3. **Automated Cut/Scene Detection Limits:** Algorithmic scene detection relies on color histogram and pixel delta shifts. Rapid camera pans, sudden camera flashes, or passing headlights will trigger false scene-change alerts.

### 4. Audio Forensic Analysis: Spectrograms & Acoustic DSP

#### 4.1 Technical Mechanism
The audio engine decodes containers into raw Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) streams and computes Root Mean Square (RMS) energy envelopes, Zero Crossing Rates (ZCR), and Short-Time Fourier Transforms (STFT) displayed as Mel-frequency spectrograms.

#### 4.2 Inherent Limitations & Acoustic Caveats
1. **Psychoacoustic Masking & High-Frequency Cutoffs:** Lossy audio codecs (MP3, AAC, OGG) discard frequency components deemed inaudible to human hearing. For example, standard 128 kbps MP3 files exhibit a sharp spectral brick-wall cutoff at approximately 16 kHz. This frequency shelf is a natural characteristic of MP3 encoding, **not** evidence that the recording was band-passed to conceal acoustic anomalies.
2. **Acoustic Discontinuities vs Background Shifts:** Ambient noise changes (such as an air conditioner cycling on/off or passing traffic) produce abrupt spectral shifts on a spectrogram that resemble splices. Purely visual spectrogram inspection cannot determine whether a background change is physical or digital.
3. **Electric Network Frequency (ENF) Constraints:** ENF analysis checks for the 50 Hz / 60 Hz hum induced by electrical grids. While valuable for authenticating timestamps, ENF analysis is **strictly invalid** unless:
   - The recording contains an audible or electromagnetic hum from mains power.
   - The examiner possesses verified, concurrent reference grid frequency recordings from the specific electrical power grid operator for the exact geographic location and date.
4. **AI Deepfakes & Neural Voice Cloning:** Generative voice models (e.g., ElevenLabs, VALL-E) produce realistic acoustic spectrograms that often pass traditional STFT analysis without visible anomalies. Specialized neural anti-spoofing classifiers (ASVspoof) are required to detect generative synthetic speech.

### 5. Metadata Fragility & Temporal Discrepancies

1. **Trivial Modifiability:** EXIF, XMP, and container metadata are plain-text or binary data structures stored in the file header. Anyone with standard hex editors or basic software can modify camera models, serial numbers, GPS coordinates, or timestamps in seconds.
2. **Metadata Absence is Not Proof of Tampering:** The fact that an image or video lacks GPS coordinates or camera serial numbers is not proof of tampering; modern operating systems (iOS, Android) and messaging apps routinely strip metadata by default for user privacy.
3. **File System vs Metadata Timestamps:**
   - *File System Timestamps (MACB):* Modified, Accessed, Created, Birth dates are maintained by the local operating system (e.g., Windows NTFS, Linux ext4). Copying a file across drives or downloading it from the web resets the Creation date to the moment of download.
   - *Internal Metadata Timestamps (EXIF DateTimeOriginal, QuickTime create_date):* Represent the clock setting of the capturing device when the file was written. If the camera clock was incorrect, the internal timestamp will be inaccurate regardless of file integrity.

### 6. Legal Admissibility Framework (FRE 901/902 & ISO/IEC 27037)

#### Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE):
- **FRE 901(a):** Requires the proponent to produce evidence sufficient to support a finding that the item is what the proponent claims it is.
- **FRE 902(13) & (14):** Certified Records Generated by an Electronic Process or System, and Certified Data Copied from an Electronic Device, Storage Medium, or File. To qualify under FRE 902(14), the digital evidence must be accompanied by a certification from a qualified examiner establishing that the hash value of the copy matches the hash value of the original.

#### ISO/IEC 27037 Principles:
1. **Auditability:** Every action taken on the evidence must be recorded so an independent third party can replicate the process and achieve identical results.
2. **Repeatability & Reproducibility:** Re-running the identical hash or analytical tool on the evidence must yield an identical mathematical outcome.
3. **Minimal Handling:** Original evidence media must never be analyzed directly; all operations must be executed on forensic bitstream copies.
